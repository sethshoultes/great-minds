# Board Review: Shonda Rhimes
**Project:** blog-board-reviews-lessons
**Deliverable:** "Patterns" blog post
**Status:** Pre-draft (planning only)

---

## Story Arc: 3/10

**No product to review yet.** Only specs and analysis docs exist.

But the **plan** reveals structural problems:

### What Should Happen (User Journey)
1. **Hook** — "Jensen asked about moats 460 times. Here's what he caught."
2. **4-Box Structure** — Jensen/Warren/Shonda/Oprah sections
3. **Patterns Section** — Where they agree/disagree
4. **Limitations** — What AI boards miss
5. **CTA** — "Try this yourself"

### What's Missing
- **No narrative tension between sections.** Four isolated portraits, no throughline.
- **No "aha moment" reveal.** Pattern: readers see what's coming from mile one.
- **Episodic, not serialized.** Each section = complete unit. No "wait, there's more."

**The plan treats this like a **reference doc**, not a **story**.**

Reference docs don't get shared. Stories do.

## Retention Hooks: 2/10

### Tomorrow Hook: **ABSENT**
Nothing brings reader back day after review.

No sequel teased. No "Part 2 drops Friday." No promise of ongoing series.

One-and-done content.

### Next Week Hook: **WEAK**
Spec mentions "tweetable stats" and "newsletter excerpt."

But no **mechanical hook** in content itself. No:
- Downloadable template of board questions
- Interactive scorecard ("Rate your pitch")
- Email course: "Run your idea through Jensen's lens"

Reader finishes, closes tab, forgets.

### Next Month Hook: **MISSING**
Zero subscription mechanism tied to content.

No:
- "Get new patterns monthly"
- "Board review your pitch (waitlist)"
- "AI board for your product (beta)"

**This is a **service business** model (Great Minds board reviews), but content strategy = **episodic blog post**.**

No flywheel connecting content → reader → customer → content.

## Content Strategy: 4/10

### What Works
- **Real quotes from 175 reviews** — Social proof at scale
- **Quantified patterns** — "Jensen asked about moats in 165/175 reviews"
- **Honest limitations section** — Builds credibility

### What Doesn't
**No content flywheel.**

Current plan:
1. Write blog post
2. Ship it
3. Hope for 1000+ views
4. ???

**Where's the participation layer?**

Compare to what *could* exist:
- Reader submits pitch → Gets AI board review → Shares results → Drives traffic
- Readers vote on "worst moat fail" → Community engagement → Newsletter signups
- Template: "Run your idea through Jensen's 4 questions" → Lead gen → Product funnel

**Spec says:**
> "If v1 gets 1000+ views, THEN invest in visual design for v2"

**Missing question:** What turns those 1000 readers into *contributors*, not just *consumers*?

**Current strategy = broadcast. Retention requires **conversation**.**

## Emotional Cliffhangers: 1/10

### The Hook (Planned)
> "We ran 11 board reviews with AI versions of Jensen Huang, Warren Buffett, Oprah Winfrey, and Shonda Rhimes. Here's what they always catch — and what they always miss."

**Informative. Not a cliffhanger.**

It *tells* the answer in the subhead. Zero suspense.

### What a Cliffhanger Would Be
> "Jensen Huang rejected 95% of our pitches for the same reason. We didn't see it until review #48."

Or:

> "Oprah gave our infrastructure project 8/10. Jensen gave it 2/10. They were both right."

**Cliffhanger = unanswered question reader needs resolved.**

Current hook = preview of entire article. No mystery.

### Section Transitions (Planned)
Four discrete sections (Jensen/Warren/Shonda/Oprah).

No transitions that build tension:
- "Jensen caught the moat problem. But Warren saw something darker..."
- "All four agreed this project should ship. Then Shonda read the retention plan."

**Sections = Wikipedia entries, not TV episodes.**

### The Ending (Planned)
CTA: "Try this yourself" with link to Great Minds.

**No cliffhanger for next post. No unanswered question. No "to be continued."**

Reader reaches end, satisfied. Satisfaction ≠ return visit.

**Retention requires **dissatisfaction** — the good kind. "I need to know what happens next."**

## Patterns I'm Catching (Meta)

### 1. Missing Product Integration
Blog post exists **outside** product experience.

Should be **inside** it:
- Post ends with: "Review your pitch now (2 min)"
- Results page shows: "See what our board caught in 175 other reviews"
- Cycle continues.

### 2. Broadcast, Not Participation
Reader = passive consumer.

No:
- Vote on best quote
- Submit pitch snippet
- Join waitlist for human board review
- Download scorecard template

**One-way content = weak retention.**

### 3. Episodic Framing
Spec calls this "blog post," not "series launch."

**Episode 1 of what?**

Should be:
- "Patterns #1: What AI Boards Catch"
- "Patterns #2: The Moat Fails We Almost Shipped" (next week)
- "Patterns #3: When Oprah and Jensen Disagree" (week 3)

**Serialization = retention.**

### 4. No "What's Next?" Tease
Post ends cleanly.

Should end with:
- "Next week: The 3 fatal flaws all four board members missed"
- "Tomorrow: Jensen's secret question that killed 40% of our pitches"

**Cliffhanger = return visit.**

## What Would Make This 7+/10

### Story Arc Fixes
- **Throughline:** Not "Here are 4 reviewers." Instead: "We thought we understood quality. 175 reviews later, here's what we learned."
- **Reveal structure:** Start with divergence (same project, scores 2-8/10), build to "why this matters"
- **Narrative payoff:** End with synthesis, not summary

### Retention Hooks
- **Tomorrow:** Part 2 announcement ("The review that made us kill a product")
- **Next week:** Interactive tool ("Score your pitch using Jensen's framework")
- **Next month:** Email course ("AI Board Review Bootcamp — 4 weeks")

### Content Flywheel
- **Participation:** "Submit your pitch. We'll review 10 reader submissions next week."
- **Community:** Readers vote on "most brutal Jensen quote"
- **Product integration:** Post → pitch submission → results → sharing → traffic

### Emotional Cliffhangers
- **Opening:** Unanswered question ("One project scored 2, 4, 6, 8 from the same board. All four were right. Here's how.")
- **Transitions:** "Jensen caught the missing moat. But he missed something Oprah saw in 3 seconds."
- **Ending:** "Next time: The limitation all four reviewers share — and the human question they'll never ask."
