## Unit Economics: What does it cost to acquire and serve one user?

**Content production cost:**
- 175 board reviews analyzed (700 persona evaluations total)
- Estimated 40+ hours of LLM compute across reviews
- Manual curation: ~8 hours (analysis.md creation)
- Blog drafting: est. 4-6 hours

**Per-reader acquisition cost:**
- Zero paid distribution (organic only)
- Distribution: existing audience, SEO, word-of-mouth
- **Effective CAC: $0** if blog generates inbound interest

**Serving cost:**
- Static markdown served via existing infrastructure
- Marginal cost per reader: ~$0

**Economics verdict:** High fixed cost to produce (50+ hours), zero marginal cost to distribute. Classic content marketing. Business case depends entirely on conversion.

**Direct revenue:** $0
Blog posts don't charge admission.

**Indirect revenue potential:**

**The uncomfortable truth:**
If you don't know your unit economics, you're not running a business—you're running a science experiment with someone else's money.

**This blog post is marketing spend, not a revenue product.**

Worth it IF:
- CTA converts readers to paying clients
- 1000+ views generates 10+ qualified leads

Not worth it IF:
- No conversion tracking on CTA
- No follow-up mechanism for interested readers

**Revenue model assessment:** Content marketing investment. ROI depends on lead conversion rate, which is **currently unmeasured**.

## Competitive Moat: What stops someone from copying this in a weekend?

**Copyable in a weekend:**
- Blog post format
- "AI board review" concept
- 4-persona structure

**Not copyable:**
- 175 real reviews worth of source data
- Actual quotes from shipped projects
- Institutional knowledge embedded in analysis

**Moat depth: Moderate**

The format is trivial to copy. The **data corpus** (175 reviews, 700+ persona evaluations) is the moat. Competitors can spin up AI personas instantly. They cannot retroactively review 175 projects.

**But:**
- Data moat decays daily (competitors ship reviews too)
- First-mover advantage in "AI board review patterns" lasts ~6 months
- Post value is time-sensitive: "11 board reviews" becomes "175 reviews" (already happened)

**Charlie Munger principle applies:**
If your business can be replicated over a weekend, it's not a business—it's a feature.

This blog post is a feature of the Great Minds brand. The moat is **reputation + data corpus**, not the content itself.

**Strategic question:**
What compounds over time?
- Blog post alone: nothing
- Blog post + ongoing review corpus + pattern updates: **data flywheel**

If Great Minds publishes quarterly updates ("What 500 reviews taught us"), the moat widens. If this is one-and-done, moat is paper-thin.

## Capital Efficiency: Are we spending wisely?

**Investment:**
- 50+ hours of human + LLM time
- Opportunity cost: could have shipped 2-3 client projects instead

**What we're buying:**
- SEO authority ("AI board review patterns")
- Inbound lead generation
- Trust signal for potential clients
- Reference content for AI builder community

**Is this the right allocation?**

Ask yourself: **If you had $10,000 and one week, would you spend it writing blog posts or building revenue-generating products?**

Depends on current bottleneck:
- **If problem is awareness:** Blog post is correct spend
- **If problem is conversion:** Better to optimize existing funnel
- **If problem is product-market fit:** Build, don't blog

**My concern:**
PRD says "Zero revenue. No lead generation funnel. No conversion path."

We're writing content to attract leads, but **where do they go after reading?**

CTA better link to:
- Free board review demo
- Email signup for pattern updates
- Consultation booking calendar

Otherwise, we've spent 50 hours creating **a cost center disguised as a deliverable**.

## Capital Efficiency Note: The $40 Test

For $40/month more, you could run paid LinkedIn ads targeting "AI founders" and drive 500+ qualified impressions.

For $0/month, you wrote a blog post that might organically reach 1000 readers over 6 months.

**Paid vs. organic tradeoff:**
Organic takes longer but compounds. Paid is instant but stops when budget ends.

This blog post is a **long-term asset** if:
- SEO ranks it for "AI board review" queries
- Gets cited/linked by AI newsletters
- Becomes reference material

It's a **sunk cost** if:
- No promotion strategy
- No follow-up content
- One reader says "cool" and closes tab

**The best investment you can make is in yourself. The second best is in infrastructure that doesn't crash at 2 AM.**

This blog post is infrastructure for reputation. Solid, if you maintain it.
