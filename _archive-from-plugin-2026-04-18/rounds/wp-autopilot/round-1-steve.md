# Steve Jobs — Chief Design & Brand Officer
**Date:** 2026-04-16
**Product:** WP Autopilot
**Round:** 1

---

## Product Naming

**The name is wrong.** "WP Autopilot" sounds like a feature, not magic. It sounds technical. It sounds forgettable.

Call it **"Wordsmith"** — one word, evocative, memorable. It speaks to what people *want* (to craft words, to build) not what the technology *does* (automation, AI, plugins). When someone asks "How'd you build that site?" they'll say "Wordsmith." Not "oh, some autopilot plugin thing."

## Design Philosophy

This is **WordPress without the manual.** The insanely great part isn't the AI — it's that the interface *disappears*.

Think of it like the iPod: 1,000 songs in your pocket wasn't about the hard drive. It was about *forgetting the hard drive exists*. Wordsmith means you forget blocks exist. You forget the plugin directory exists. You forget wp-admin's seventeen submenus exist.

You say what you want. It appears. **That's it.**

The design must be invisible. A white chat panel. Clean typography. No gradients, no "AI" branding, no robot icons. Just a conversation with your website.

## User Experience: The First 30 Seconds

You install Wordsmith. A panel slides open in wp-admin. **One sentence at the top:**

> **"What would you like to build?"**

Not "Welcome to Wordsmith!" Not a tutorial. Not feature highlights. Just the question.

You type: *"Add a contact page."*

Three seconds later: *"I've created a Contact page with a form. Want to see it?"* — with a blue link to the page.

**That's the experience.** No loading spinners with "AI is thinking…" No jargon. No confirmation modals unless you're deleting something. Speed + clarity = magic.

## Brand Voice

Wordsmith speaks like **a confident assistant who's read your mind.**

Not: *"I can help you create a contact page using the Contact Form 7 plugin, which I'll install and configure for you. Would you like to proceed?"*

Instead: *"Done. Your contact form is live and emails go to seth@yoursite.com."*

Short sentences. Active voice. No hedging ("might," "possibly," "I think"). No corporate speak ("leverage," "utilize"). No AI cheerleading ("As an AI assistant…").

**Confident. Human. Direct.**

## What to Say NO To

**Say NO to:**
1. **Settings.** No "advanced mode." No checkboxes for "verbose output" or "confirm every action." Settings are a designer's admission of failure.
2. **Branding inside the chat.** No "Powered by Claude" footer. No Wordsmith logo on every message. The product is the experience, not the badge.
3. **Marketplace upsells.** No "Upgrade to Pro for WooCommerce support!" in the MVP. Monetization later. Magic first.
4. **Undo buttons next to every action.** Trust the AI. If it messes up, they'll *tell you* to fix it. Don't pre-apologize with UI clutter.

**Simplicity means removing.** Every modal, every setting, every safety net is a vote of no-confidence in your own product.

## The Emotional Hook

People will **LOVE** this because it makes them feel **powerful without feeling stupid.**

Right now, WordPress makes you feel dumb. "Where's the block inserter?" "Why didn't the form plugin work?" "What's a slug?" Wordsmith flips that. You say what you want — in plain English, with typos, with vague descriptions — and it works.

**It's not about saving time. It's about feeling capable.**

When someone uses Wordsmith to build a page in 30 seconds that would've taken them 30 minutes of Googling and frustration, they don't think "cool AI." They think **"I'm actually good at this."**

That's the emotion. Confidence. The same feeling you get when the iPhone keyboard guesses the right word. It's not serving you — it's making *you* smarter.

**Build this. Make it invisible. Make it instant. Make people feel like they finally understand WordPress — not because we taught them, but because we made it irrelevant to know.**
